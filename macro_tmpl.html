NIFTY and the US: regimes, yield spreads and what comes next

Bond-yield spreads do not explain when NIFTY moves with the US. The India–US 10-year gap shrank from about 5.6 points in 2015 to 2.4 in 2026 while the correlation stayed around 0.3. Our best forecast is the long-run average, and it beats the regime and HAR models we tried.

Monthly data, February 2015 to 24 November 2026. Correlation is NIFTY daily return against the prior S&P 500 session. Sources: Yahoo Finance, US Treasury, FRED (OECD India yields, monthly averages to October 2026).

Our view

  • Base case: a weak positive link returns. The next-month correlation forecast is 0.28, with an 80% range of −0.09 to 0.60. The chance of a negative month is about 18%.
  • A negative 12-month correlation has no precedent here. No 12-month window since 2015 was below zero.
  • Current regime: Neutral, just out of Squeeze. September and October were Squeeze (US yields and oil up). November has one trigger active, the dollar, and needs two.
  • Yield spreads are visible but not predictive. None of 50 spread and yield tests reached 5% significance.
  • One new feature in 2026: NIFTY has fallen when US 10-year yields rose overnight (correlation −0.25), the strongest such link in 12 years.

The whole picture

Read down a vertical line to see one month across every panel. The coloured strip is the macro regime.

NIFTY's relative performance peaked in September 2022 and has fallen on balance since, while the India–US spread kept narrowing. That overlap is one slow trend, not repeated evidence. The sections below test whether changes line up quarter by quarter.

Every test

Correlation across 46–47 quarters. Blue is positive, red is negative. Two-sided p under 0.10 is marked with an asterisk; with 50 tests, about five would be expected by chance.

The nearest results are a steeper US 3-month to 10-year curve with NIFTY lagging in the same quarter (−0.28, p = 0.06), and a rise in the US 10-year followed by NIFTY outperforming the next quarter (+0.27, p = 0.06). Neither survives the number of tests.

Fundamental context, and where to dig

Our data shows Brent closing March 2026 at $118, USDINR moving from about 87 on average in 2025 to 94 in 2026, and the US 10-year ending September at 5.3%. Press coverage ties 2026's underperformance to record foreign selling during the oil shock and reports the India–US spread widening to 251 bp in October. We have not verified those reports or tested flows.

  • Foreign and domestic flows. Daily NSDL foreign flows and mutual-fund SIP data could explain why domestic money blunted the US link.
  • Index composition. NIFTY has little AI hardware. Compare Taiwan and KOSPI, which stayed linked.
  • Earnings and valuation. Test whether the earnings-growth gap explains relative return better than yields do.
  • Daily Indian yields. Monthly averages are too coarse for event studies around RBI and Fed meetings.
  • Risk dashboard. Use the regime as a displayed label beside volatility forecasts. Its forecasting test failed, so it should not change position size.

Limits

  • 142 months and 47 quarters is a small sample. Failing to find an effect does not prove there is none: correlations below roughly 0.3 would often be missed.
  • The regime rule set is our own choice. Others could classify some months differently.
  • Some outcomes for 2026 were seen in earlier work before these regimes were defined. The thresholds were not tuned to them.
