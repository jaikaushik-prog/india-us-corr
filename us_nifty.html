Is NIFTY's correlation with the US turning negative?

Mostly no. NIFTY has drifted away from the US in 2026, and its daily link to the S&P 500 has been weak since July. Much of the sharper change comes from November 2026, when NIFTY rose as the US fell. It still does not tend to fall when the US rises.

Correlation runs from −1 to +1. At +1 two markets always move together, at 0 they ignore each other, and at −1 they move opposite ways. The dots show NIFTY against the S&P 500, comparing each NIFTY day with the US session that finished before it.

Two ways to measure it give two answers

Price levels ask whether the two markets trended the same way over the year. Daily returns ask whether they moved the same way each day. Two markets can agree most days and still finish the year apart.

Trend over the year: negative in 2026

2026 is the first negative year since at least 2015. From 2015 to 2025 this ranged between 0.18 and 0.93. The 2026 figures are −0.36 for the S&P, −0.42 for Nasdaq and −0.09 for the Dow. In US dollars NIFTY's S&P figure is −0.54, so the rupee made the gap wider. Trending prices can easily give large readings of either sign.

Day by day: still positive every year

The line through each dot is the 95% range. 2022 and 2023 were the two highest years, so a chart that starts in 2023 makes the fall look bigger than it is. 2024 to 2026 (0.18 to 0.26) sit just under the 0.27 to 0.31 of 2015 to 2018. Their ranges overlap too much to rank them, 2025 just includes zero, and 2019 was effectively zero at 0.01.

2025 also leans on one event. Without the April tariff shock (3 to 15 April), that year's S&P correlation drops from 0.26 to 0.04.

NIFTY usually follows a big US move

If NIFTY moved opposite to the US, it would fall after strong US days. It does the reverse. The blue squares show how often, out of 100, NIFTY closed higher in the session after each kind of US day since 2023.

NIFTY gained 0.54% on average after US rises above 1.5%, and lost 0.49% after falls of more than 1.5%. Part of that can happen at the open, before anyone can trade it, so this is no trading result.

The link is stronger when markets are nervous

VIX measures how much fear is priced into US stocks. We split the days since 2023 into three equal groups by VIX.

So diversifying between India and the US may help least in a scare. April 2025 drives part of this: without it, the high-VIX figure is 0.25. The same order appeared in 2015 to 2022 (0.21, 0.24, 0.32), but the ranges overlap.

Longer holding periods look weaker only with November

Measuring returns over a month of trading (21 sessions) reduces the time-zone problem. On that basis the correlation fell from 0.59 in 2023 to 0.22 in 2026. Without November 2026 it is 0.63, so the fall depends on that month.

Columns are the number of sessions each return covers, ending on the same date. Long returns overlap, so they hold few independent observations. Without November, 2026 reads 0.24, 0.36, 0.51, 0.57 and 0.63.

Who leads, the US or India?

The US. The previous US session is the one column that is clearly positive every year. NIFTY's own past moves say little about later US sessions.

Weekly and quarterly: no lasting decoupling

Our rule was that a decoupling trend must be significant whether it starts in 2023 or in 2015. Neither weekly nor quarterly data met it for the S&P.

What the period figures show

By year the S&P went 0.40, 0.18, 0.26, 0.25. Half-years swing between about 0 and 0.45 with no clear direction. In the second half of 2026 the S&P, Nasdaq and Dow are at 0.02, 0.00 and 0.09, after 0.43, 0.39 and 0.42 in the first half.

On price levels the S&P went 0.91, 0.79, 0.76, −0.36, and the second half of 2026 is −0.58. Nasdaq is similar and the Dow is weaker, at 0.09 in that half. Most of the yearly change comes from 2026 alone.

Same-date returns in November 2026 are sharply negative (−0.61, −0.61 and −0.53) in an incomplete month. The three months before it were positive.

No trend in returns is significant at any frequency. On levels, the monthly downtrend is significant at 5% for the S&P and Nasdaq (one-sided p about 0.03 and 0.02) but not for the Dow (0.45). Eight half-years and four years are too few for a strong test.

Method and limits
  • NIFTY closes at 15:30 IST, before the US opens. "US session first" pairs each NIFTY return with the US sessions that closed since NIFTY's previous close. "Same date" asks whether the US followed NIFTY.
  • Each period figure is the Pearson correlation of daily log returns, or of daily closes for price levels, inside that period. Periods do not overlap and prices are in local currency.
  • Trend p-values come from shuffling the order of periods 4,000 times, with no adjustment for the number of tests. Yearly ranges use a bootstrap with 5-session blocks.
  • Other Asian markets use their own closes and the US sessions since their previous close.
  • With this many cuts of the same data, some differences will be chance. We tested no strategies and included no trading costs.
  • Yahoo lacks five special NIFTY sessions (Muhurat, Saturday and Budget sessions).
