Can yields, oil and the dollar explain when NIFTY follows the US?

Very little, as far as we can measure. The gap between Indian and US bond yields has more than halved since 2015 and NIFTY's link to the S&P 500 barely changed. We expect a weak positive link next month, close to its long-run average.

"Link" means the correlation between NIFTY's daily return and the S&P 500 session that finished before it. +1 is in step, 0 is no link and −1 is opposite. Data runs from February 2015 to 24 November 2026.

What we expect next month

Our forecast is 0.28, with an 80% range of −0.09 to 0.60. Out of 100 months like this, we would expect the link to be negative in about 18 (red).

A negative link lasting a full year would be a first: no 12-month window since 2015 has been below zero.

Ranges for 3, 6 and 12 months

The next-month range uses the errors of past forecasts. The longer ranges are the 10th to 90th percentiles of overlapping history since 2015, so they describe the past and are not independent forecasts.

The yield gap narrowed and the link did not follow

The top line is India's 10-year bond yield minus the US one. It fell from about 5.6 points in 2015 to 2.4 in 2026. The bottom line is NIFTY's link to the S&P over six months, which kept swinging around 0.3.

The coloured strip shows the macro regime each month, explained below. Hover or tap to read a month.

50 checks, none significant

We compared ten yield measures with five NIFTY outcomes, in the same quarter and the next. Each square is one check and would be red if it passed the usual 5% test. None did.

With 47 quarters of data, an effect below roughly 0.3 would often be missed, so this does not prove there is none.

See every check

Correlation across 46 or 47 quarters. Blue is positive and red is negative. An asterisk marks p below 0.10; chance alone would produce about five of those in 50, and we found three.

Two came closest. NIFTY lagged in quarters when the US 3-month to 10-year curve steepened (−0.28, p = 0.06), and beat the S&P in the quarter after US 10-year yields rose (+0.27, p = 0.06). Neither survives the number of tests.

India-US yield gap against NIFTY's relative return

Each dot is a quarter: change in the gap (points, across) and NIFTY minus S&P return (%, up). A real effect would form a slope. Correlation −0.16, p = 0.28.

All five panels: performance, link, yield gap, US curve, US 10-year

NIFTY's performance relative to the S&P peaked in September 2022 and has fallen on balance since, while the yield gap kept narrowing. That overlap is one slow trend and cannot count as repeated evidence.

One change in 2026: NIFTY reacts to US yields

NIFTY tended to fall after US 10-year yields rose. The correlation is −0.25, the most negative in 12 years. Bars below zero mean NIFTY fell when yields rose.

The S&P showed the same sign in 2026 (−0.18), which points to a global rates effect. It is one year out of 12, too few to call a pattern.

Where we are now: Neutral, one trigger from Squeeze

A Squeeze month needs two of four pressures to pass their limits over three months. In November only the dollar has. September and October were Squeeze months, with US yields and oil over the limit in September and oil and the dollar in October.

Each bar is the three-month change as a share of its limit. The limits are US 10-year +0.25 points, dollar index +2%, Brent oil +15% and USDINR +2%. USDINR falling means the rupee strengthened.

The link is strongest in Stress and weakest in Squeeze, where NIFTY also lagged the S&P by 0.5 points a month. Background and where to dig next

Brent closed March 2026 at $118. USDINR moved from about 87 on average in 2025 to 94 in 2026, and the US 10-year ended September at 5.3%. Press reports tie NIFTY's 2026 lag to record foreign selling during the oil shock, and say the India-US gap widened to 251 bp in October. We have not verified those reports or tested flows.

  • Do foreign flows (NSDL) and mutual-fund SIPs explain why domestic money blunted the US link?
  • Does sector mix matter? NIFTY has little AI hardware, while Taiwan and Korea stayed linked.
  • Does the gap in earnings growth or valuation explain relative returns better than yields?
  • Do daily Indian yields show effects around RBI and Fed meetings? Ours are monthly averages.
  • On the risk dashboard, the regime can be shown as a label beside volatility forecasts. It failed its forecasting test, so it should not change position size.
Method and limits
  • Sources are Yahoo Finance, US Treasury and FRED (OECD Indian yields, monthly averages to October 2026).
  • Regime limits were set before looking at outcomes. Triggers are three-month changes at month-end. Some 2026 outcomes had been seen in earlier work, but the limits were not tuned to them.
  • Ranges in the regime chart are 95% intervals from resampling months. There are 142 months and 47 quarters.
  • Other reasonable regime rules would classify some months differently.
